📐 Mathematics

AP Calculus AB / BC

A college-level calculus course. AB covers the fundamentals; BC adds series, polar, and parametric content. Strong algebra and trigonometry skills are essential before you start.

📅 Exam: May ⏱ 3 hours 15 min Difficulty: Hard ~60% pass rate (score 3+)

📚 Core Topics

Limits & Continuity

Definition of a limit, one-sided limits, limits at infinity, L'Hôpital's Rule for indeterminate forms, and the three conditions for continuity at a point (defined, limit exists, limit = value).

Derivatives

Limit definition of the derivative, power rule, product rule, quotient rule, chain rule, implicit differentiation, and derivatives of trig, exponential, and logarithmic functions.

Applications of Derivatives

Related rates, optimization using critical points, Mean Value Theorem, curve sketching (increasing/decreasing, concavity), and particle motion (position, velocity, acceleration).

Integrals & the Fundamental Theorem

Riemann sums, definite and indefinite integrals, u-substitution, FTC Part 1 (derivative of an integral) and Part 2 (evaluation using antiderivatives), and area between curves.

Differential Equations

Slope fields, separation of variables, exponential growth and decay models (dy/dt = ky). Know how to sketch and interpret slope fields from a given differential equation.

BC Only: Series & More

Sequences and series, convergence tests (integral, comparison, ratio, alternating series), Taylor and Maclaurin series, parametric equations, polar coordinates, and arc length.

💡 Study Tips

  • Memorize your derivative and integral rules cold — power rule, trig derivatives, ln/e rules. These appear constantly and you cannot afford to slow down on them.
  • Practice free-response questions daily. FRQs are 50% of your score and require showing every step clearly — partial credit is real, so write out your work even if you're unsure.
  • Use the College Board's released free-response questions from the past 10 years. They're free online and are the most accurate representation of what you'll see.
  • Know when to use your calculator and when not to. In calculator sections, use it for definite integrals, intersection points, and numerical derivatives. Don't waste time doing these by hand.
  • For BC students: series is its own world — start early. Learn convergence tests in order: divergence test first, then integral, comparison, ratio. Understand which test to apply and when.
