AP English Language

Rhetoric, argumentation, and analytical writing. Three essays on exam day: synthesis, rhetorical analysis, and argument.

📅 Exam: May ⏱ 3 hr 15 min 🔥 Difficulty: Medium ~55% pass rate
⚡ Exam Tips
  • Never just label a device — always explain the EFFECT it creates on the reader
  • A vague thesis caps your score at a 3 — name strategies AND explain how they achieve purpose
  • Synthesis: pick a side quickly. You have limited time — don't try to be neutral
  • Argument essay: address a counterargument to earn higher scores
  • Read one op-ed daily leading up to the exam — trains fast rhetorical analysis

📚 Core Topics
What's on the Exam
  • Rhetorical Situation
    Purpose, audience, genre, occasion — every choice the author makes connects back to these
  • Claims, Evidence & Reasoning
    Claim → specific evidence → reasoning that connects evidence back to claim (don't skip reasoning)
  • Rhetorical Appeals (Ethos/Logos/Pathos)
    Identify which appeal is used and explain WHY the author chose it — effect on the audience
  • Style & Tone
    Diction, syntax, figurative language — analyze the effect of specific word and structure choices
  • Synthesis Essay
    Form your own argument using 3+ of 6–7 provided sources. Don't summarize — use sources as evidence
  • Argument Essay
    Take a clear position, use real-world evidence, address a counterargument, show nuanced reasoning

💡 Study Tips
How to Prepare
  • Write a timed essay every week — set 40 minutes, write the full essay, then score it with the rubric
  • Your thesis is everything — practice writing it in under 2 minutes: specific claim + line of reasoning
  • Never just label a rhetorical device — "they use anaphora" earns nothing. Explain the effect it creates
  • For synthesis: spend 5 min reading, 5 min outlining, 30 min writing — don't overthink source selection
