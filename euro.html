🏰 History & Social Studies

AP European History

European history from 1450 to the present. Covers the Renaissance, Reformation, Scientific Revolution, Enlightenment, revolutions, nationalism, world wars, and the Cold War. Writing skills are essential.

📅 Exam: May ⏱ 3 hours 15 min Difficulty: Hard ~55% pass rate (score 3+)

📚 The 4 Major Periods

Period 1 (1450–1648): Renaissance to Religious Wars

The Italian Renaissance — humanism, secularism, and new artistic ideals (Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael). The printing press and its role in spreading ideas. The Protestant Reformation: Luther's 95 Theses, Calvin's predestination, the Catholic Counter-Reformation (Council of Trent, Jesuits). The Scientific Revolution: Copernicus (heliocentric theory), Galileo, Kepler, and Francis Bacon's empiricism. The Age of Exploration and European colonialism. The Thirty Years' War (1618–1648) and the Peace of Westphalia establishing state sovereignty.

Period 2 (1648–1815): Absolutism to Revolution

Absolutism: Louis XIV ("L'état, c'est moi"), Versailles as a tool of political control, Peter the Great's westernization of Russia. The Enlightenment: Locke (natural rights, social contract), Voltaire (religious tolerance), Montesquieu (separation of powers), Rousseau (general will). The French Revolution: causes (debt, inequality, Estates), key phases (Constitutional Monarchy → Reign of Terror → Directory → Napoleon). Napoleon's rise, the Napoleonic Code, wars of expansion, and eventual defeat. The Congress of Vienna restoring conservative order.

Period 3 (1815–1914): Industrialization & Nationalism

The Industrial Revolution — Britain first, then the continent. Factory system, urbanization, child labor, and the rise of the working class. Liberalism vs. conservatism vs. socialism (Marx and Engels' Communist Manifesto, 1848). The Revolutions of 1848 — causes and why most failed. Italian unification (Cavour, Garibaldi) and German unification (Bismarck, "blood and iron"). The new imperialism: European powers carving up Africa and Asia. The buildup to WWI: alliance system, militarism, nationalism (especially in the Balkans), assassination of Franz Ferdinand.

Period 4 (1914–Present): Wars, Totalitarianism & Recovery

World War I: trench warfare, total war, the role of new weapons (gas, tanks, planes), the Treaty of Versailles and its harsh terms. The Russian Revolution: causes, February vs. October Revolution, Lenin and the Bolsheviks, Stalin's rise and totalitarian state. Rise of fascism: Mussolini in Italy, Hitler in Germany (causes, ideology, the Holocaust). World War II: appeasement, Blitzkrieg, the Eastern Front, D-Day, and the Holocaust. The Cold War: Marshall Plan, NATO, Berlin Wall, decolonization, détente. European integration: the EU, fall of the Soviet Union (1991), and challenges of the post-Cold War era.

✍️ DBQ & LEQ Skills

ThesisA historically defensible claim with a line of reasoning — not a simple restatement of the prompt
ContextualizationDescribe the broader historical context BEFORE your period, then connect it to your argument
Document EvidenceUse content from at least 6 of 7 documents; go beyond summary to explain significance
Sourcing (HAPPy)Analyze Historical situation, Audience, Purpose, or Point of view for at least 3 documents
Outside EvidenceInclude at least one piece of evidence not in the documents to earn the outside evidence point
ComplexityShow nuance — corroboration across docs, tension within the period, or cause/effect across time

💡 Study Tips

  • Think in themes, not just dates. AP Euro tests causation and comparison. For every event ask: what caused it? What were the effects? How does it compare to a similar event in a different period? Connections earn points.
  • Master the DBQ rubric before the exam. Know exactly what earns each of the 7 points — thesis, contextualization, evidence (content), evidence (sourcing × 3), and outside evidence. Practice writing one DBQ per week under timed conditions.
  • Contextualization is the hardest point to earn and students most often miss it. It requires describing the broader historical situation BEFORE your period, then explicitly connecting it to your argument — not just mentioning it.
  • Build a mental map of the four periods. Know the major turning points: 1517 (Reformation), 1648 (Westphalia), 1789 (French Revolution), 1848 (Revolutions), 1914 (WWI), 1939 (WWII), 1991 (Cold War ends). Everything connects to these anchors.
  • Practice reading primary sources quickly. The multiple choice, SAQs, and DBQ all require you to analyze documents — speeches, letters, political cartoons, maps. The faster you can identify an author's argument and historical context, the better your score.
