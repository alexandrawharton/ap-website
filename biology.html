🧬 Science

AP Biology

A college-level biology course emphasizing scientific inquiry and reasoning. Covers four major themes: evolution, cellular processes, genetics, and ecology. Expect to analyze data and design experiments.

📅 Exam: May ⏱ 3 hours Difficulty: Hard ~65% pass rate (score 3+)

Score WeightingMultiple Choice = 50% · Free Response = 50%

📚 Core Topics

Cell Structure & Function

Prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic cells, organelle functions, the fluid mosaic model of the cell membrane, membrane transport (diffusion, osmosis, active transport), and cell communication via signal transduction.

Cellular Energetics

Cellular respiration (glycolysis, Krebs cycle, ETC), photosynthesis (light reactions, Calvin cycle), enzyme function and regulation, and the role of ATP as the cell's energy currency.

DNA, Gene Expression & Regulation

DNA replication, transcription, translation (the central dogma), gene regulation in prokaryotes (operons) and eukaryotes (epigenetics), mutations, and biotechnology tools (PCR, gel electrophoresis, CRISPR).

Cell Division & Genetics

Mitosis vs. meiosis, Mendelian genetics, non-Mendelian inheritance (codominance, incomplete dominance, sex-linkage, polygenic traits), and chi-square analysis to test genetic hypotheses.

Evolution & Natural Selection

Darwin's theory, Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (and its five conditions), genetic drift, gene flow, speciation, phylogenetics, and the evidence for evolution (fossil record, comparative anatomy, molecular biology).

Ecology

Population dynamics (logistic vs. exponential growth, carrying capacity), community interactions (competition, predation, symbiosis), energy flow through ecosystems (10% rule), and biogeochemical cycles.

ATPAdenosine triphosphate — the cell's universal energy currency
Natural SelectionHeritable variation + differential reproductive success
MeiosisCell division producing haploid gametes; source of genetic variation
OsmosisWater movement across a membrane from low to high solute concentration
EnzymeBiological catalyst; lowers activation energy of a reaction
Carrying CapacityMaximum population a habitat can sustainably support
OperonGroup of genes regulated together in prokaryotes (e.g., lac operon)
Genetic DriftRandom changes in allele frequency, especially in small populations

💡 Study Tips

  • Understand processes, not just definitions. For cellular respiration, don't memorize the steps — understand WHY each step happens and what it produces. Examiners love "explain" questions.
  • Draw and label everything. Cell diagrams, the lac operon, Hardy-Weinberg graphs — drawing forces you to understand the structure. It also prepares you to sketch diagrams in FRQ answers.
  • Practice chi-square analysis. You will almost certainly get a genetics data question requiring you to calculate chi-square, compare to a critical value, and state whether you reject the null hypothesis.
  • Learn the Hardy-Weinberg equation (p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1) and practice calculating allele and genotype frequencies from population data.
  • Read the FRQ prompts carefully — they often contain data tables or graphs you must analyze. State your claim, cite the data, and explain the mechanism. Don't skip the "explain" part.
