📚 Core Topics

Biological Bases of Behavior

Neurons, the synapse, and neurotransmitters (dopamine, serotonin, acetylcholine, GABA). Brain structures and their functions: frontal lobe (decision-making), hippocampus (memory), amygdala (emotion), cerebellum (coordination). The nervous system divisions and the endocrine system.

Sensation & Perception

How sensory organs detect stimuli (transduction) and how the brain interprets them. Key concepts: absolute threshold, difference threshold (JND), signal detection theory, Weber's Law, perceptual constancy, and depth cues. Know the visual pathway from retina to occipital lobe.

States of Consciousness

Sleep stages (NREM 1–3 and REM), circadian rhythms, sleep disorders (insomnia, narcolepsy, sleep apnea). Psychoactive drugs: depressants (alcohol, barbiturates), stimulants (caffeine, cocaine), hallucinogens (LSD), and opiates — know their effects and how they alter neurotransmission.

Learning

Classical conditioning (Pavlov): US, UR, CS, CR, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, discrimination. Operant conditioning (Skinner): positive/negative reinforcement and punishment, schedules of reinforcement (fixed ratio, variable ratio, fixed interval, variable interval). Observational learning (Bandura's Bobo doll study).

Memory

Encoding, storage, and retrieval. The three-stage model: sensory memory → short-term memory (7±2 items) → long-term memory. Explicit (declarative) vs. implicit (procedural) memory. Forgetting causes: decay, interference (proactive and retroactive), repression. The role of the hippocampus in forming new long-term memories.

Cognition, Language & Intelligence

Problem-solving strategies (algorithms vs. heuristics), cognitive biases (confirmation bias, availability heuristic, anchoring). Language acquisition (Chomsky's language acquisition device). Intelligence theories: Spearman's g factor, Gardner's multiple intelligences, Sternberg's triarchic theory. IQ testing history and its controversies.

Development

Piaget's four stages of cognitive development (sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational). Erikson's eight psychosocial stages. Attachment theory (Harlow's monkeys, Ainsworth's strange situation). Kohlberg's levels of moral development.

Personality, Motivation & Emotion

Freud's psychoanalytic theory (id, ego, superego; defense mechanisms). The Big Five personality traits (OCEAN). Maslow's hierarchy of needs. Theories of emotion: James-Lange, Cannon-Bard, Schachter-Singer two-factor theory. Intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation.

Social Psychology

Attribution theory (fundamental attribution error, self-serving bias). Conformity (Asch's line study), obedience (Milgram's shock study), and groupthink. Attitudes and persuasion (cognitive dissonance, foot-in-the-door technique). Prejudice, stereotypes, and discrimination. The bystander effect and diffusion of responsibility.

Psychological Disorders & Treatment

The DSM-5 and how disorders are classified. Major disorders: depression, bipolar, schizophrenia, anxiety, OCD, PTSD, and personality disorders. Biomedical treatments (antidepressants, antipsychotics, lithium) and psychological therapies (CBT, psychoanalysis, humanistic therapy).

🔑 Must-Know Vocabulary

NeurotransmitterChemical messenger released across a synapse (e.g., dopamine, serotonin)
Classical ConditioningLearning by association — pairing a neutral stimulus with one that causes a response
Operant ConditioningLearning through consequences — reinforcement increases behavior, punishment decreases it
ReinforcementAny consequence that increases the likelihood of a behavior being repeated
Cognitive DissonanceMental discomfort from holding two conflicting beliefs; motivates attitude change
Fundamental Attribution ErrorOverestimating character, underestimating situation when explaining others' behavior
Confirmation BiasTendency to seek information that supports what you already believe
HippocampusBrain structure critical for forming new long-term explicit memories
AccommodationPiaget — adjusting existing mental schemas to incorporate new information
Bystander EffectLess likely to help in an emergency when others are present; diffusion of responsibility

💡 Study Tips

  • Make flashcards for every key term, researcher, and study. AP Psych is very vocabulary-heavy and the multiple choice will test whether you know specific terms, names, and their definitions precisely.
  • Know your landmark studies cold — Pavlov (classical conditioning), Skinner (operant), Milgram (obedience), Asch (conformity), Bandura (observational learning), Harlow (attachment). Expect to identify what each study demonstrated.
  • For FRQ 1, you'll be given a scenario and asked to apply multiple concepts to it. Practice writing clear, one-sentence answers per concept — don't ramble. Each concept earns a point if correctly applied.
  • Don't confuse similar terms. Negative reinforcement is NOT punishment — it removes something unpleasant to increase behavior. Proactive interference is old memories blocking new ones (not the other way around).
  • The last two units (disorders and treatment) are heavily tested. Learn the difference between anxiety disorders, mood disorders, and psychotic disorders — and know which neurotransmitter imbalances are associated with each.
